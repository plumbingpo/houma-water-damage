Attic Water Damage in Houma

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. For Houma homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Signs to Look For

  • Water dripping from, or staining around, a ceiling light or bathroom fan
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Wood

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Fix the Cause

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.
